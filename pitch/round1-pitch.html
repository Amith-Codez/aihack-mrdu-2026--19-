1 · Problem

A lecturer cannot hand her marking to an AI, because it marks to its own standard, not hers.

Who has the problem

An assistant professor at a JNTUH-affiliated engineering college, marking the descriptive part of a mid-term for two sections.

ASSUMED A persona built from the exam rules. We have not interviewed a lecturer yet.

20–30 min

credited per undergraduate answer script by one state's order. Madras evaluators mark about 40 scripts a day.

Why it happens

A marking scheme never covers every way a student can phrase an answer. Each marker, human or AI, fills the gaps with their own strictness.

A student needs at least 14 of 40 internal marks to sit the semester-end exam. A teacher will not sign marks she cannot check.

3 · Solution

Each loophole gets one answer.

The AI picks its own strictness

Match my marking

She marks six answers. The tool marks the same six, counts how many match her, rewrites its notes and tries again. A new round is kept only if it matches her better.

She cannot see why a mark was given

Every mark quotes the answer

Each mark shows the exact line that earned it. Code checks the line is really in the student's answer. If it is not, the mark is thrown out.

Nobody says how close the tool is

Proof on answers it has not seen

The tool marks ten answers whose real marks were held back, then shows how often it matched, beside how often two humans match each other.

The teacher loses control

She approves, always

She approves the marking notes and every mark, and can change any of them. Nothing is final without her.

We do not claim better marks than ChatGPT. We claim her standard, checked evidence, and her signature.

4 · Technology

A loop, four checks and a retry. We wrote every line.

HUMANGives the question, her scheme, the typed answers, and her marks on six.
CODEChecks the input. Splits "her six" from the unseen answers.
AGENTMatch my marking. The model marks the six; code counts matches with her; the model rewrites its notes; code keeps the round only if matches rise. At most two rounds.
HUMANReads the notes, edits them, approves.
MODELMarks the rest, five answers per call. Every mark must carry a quote.
CODEHard checks: the quote is in the answer; the marks add up. A failure is asked again once, then sent to her unmarked.
CODECompares with her real marks on the unseen answers.
HUMANEdits any mark and approves the class.

Why we call it agentic, and how far

One step is an agent: it looks at where it disagrees with her, decides what to change, tries again, and is judged by code. Everything else is fixed code or a single model call, and the screen says which.

The model cannot overrule the checks. If the loop does not beat a single rewrite in our test, we will call it a checked pipeline and say so.

What we chose and why

  • Next.js, one app. Screen and server together; one thing to deploy.
  • Our own engine, about 250 lines. A framework would hide the steps a jury asks about.
  • Checks in code, not a second AI. A text match is exact and free.
  • Gemini, with Groq as fallback. Two free providers; if one refuses a call, the other takes it.
  • No database, no login. Nothing is stored; marks stay in her browser tab.

6 · Scalability and feasibility

A new subject costs six marks, not a retrained model.

What makes it scale

  • No training per subject. A new question or a new teacher needs her scheme and six marks. This is how it is designed.
  • Nothing stored. One stateless server route; no database to grow or guard.
  • Small calls. Five answers per call, so a class of 60 is 12 calls per pass.
  • Cheap to run. 16 answers took 8 calls and 7,431 tokens. MEASURED

What limits it today

  • Free quotas. Gemini's free tier ran out after about two runs in a few minutes. Real use needs a paid key. MEASURED
  • Typed answers only. Handwritten scripts are the next step, untested.
  • One subject tested. All our numbers are from a data structures course in English.
  • Student data. We used only a public dataset. Real scripts need the college's consent and a provider that does not train on them. ASSUMED

8 · Questions we expect

Short answers to hard questions.

Why not just use ChatGPT?

It marks a single answer well. In our test the same eight answers got 21 and then 15, because it chose its own strictness each time. We fix whose standard is used, show checked evidence, and keep her approval.

What stops the AI from inventing evidence?

Every mark needs a quote that is literally in the student's answer. Plain code tests that. The model cannot overrule it, and "Break it" shows it firing.

What exactly is agentic here?

One step: Match my marking. It sees where it disagrees with the teacher, decides what to change, re-marks, and code keeps the change only if the match improves. The rest is fixed code or single model calls.

Does Match my marking actually help?

Not yet in our live run: 4 of 6 before and after, so code discarded the new notes. On unseen answers the typed scheme scored 9 of 10. We report what we measured.

Is the dataset in the model's training data?

Possibly; it is public and from 2011. That is why the judge can type an answer of their own, which the model has never seen.

What about handwritten answer sheets?

Not supported today. It is the next thing we would test, with the teacher confirming the transcript before marking.

Why your own engine and not LangChain or CrewAI?

It is about 250 lines: a loop, four checks and a retry. Every teammate can explain each step.
