# Pre-event online meeting with the organizers · 1 Oct 2026

**What this is:** the points from the organizers' pre-event meeting, as summarized by our team afterwards. The wording is second-hand: treat each point as what the organizers said, not as a published rule. The speaker's slides are in `session-deck-hacking-the-hackathon.pdf`.

## Judging
- There are no domain-level criteria and no per-domain prizes. Teams are judged on the **solution**, their **understanding of the tech they chose**, and the **pitch**.
- The **winner is decided by the average of two jury sessions**, possibly more. The jury sessions are likely online (not final).
- A UI/UX team still needs a working, minimal frontend.

## Problem statements and tools
- Problem statements come from a **provided list of 100+**. Own ideas are discouraged.
- AI tools: no restrictions.
- A community member will explain the problem statements, the platform and registration in a voice call.
- Keerthana Sree (the speaker) may visit the college.

## Team
- Teams of 5 are strongly advised. Teams of 3 will struggle.
- The whole team must understand the core logic.

## How to design the solution
- Frame: problem · impact · users · solution · top 3 features · technology.
- Map users and scenarios → inputs (file, voice) → core logic → outputs (answer, report, actions) → feedback on impact.
- Start minimal (the example given was a WhatsApp-style minimal product).

## How to run the 24 hours
- Test continuously. After the halfway point, have the jury, coordinators or outside friends try to break it.
- Teams often quit around hour 14: shrink the scope, get an MVP working, ask mentors, never quit.
- Have a working core by hour 14, then split into: UI polish · new features · bug testing.
- Back up working code before starting anything new. Park extras (the example given: voice input).

## Pitch and demo
- Hook (why this problem, how many are affected, the impact) → users → a crystal-clear live demo.
- Demo all screens, the happy path and the sad path. Show value in the first 30 seconds. Show 5–6 tested features.
- Pre-record a real, screen-recorded backup demo (not AI-generated).
- In the last seconds, ask the jury for feedback.
- Pick one confident presenter, not the team lead by default.

## From the speaker's deck (10 slides)
- Choose the problem where three circles meet: skills only you have · a real pain point · doable in 24–48 hours.
- Five questions before committing: who exactly has the problem (one person) · can I state it in one sentence · how do they solve it today and why is that painful · can I demo the fix in under 3 minutes · do I have the data or APIs right now.
- "Decoding Evaluation Secret": Problem 40% (selection, impact) · Solution 30% (prototype, proof) · Innovation 20% (unique angle, fresh) · Pitch 10% (as a team, questions).
- Winning MVP focus: a single hero feature · mocked edge cases (hard-code non-essential paths) · a flawless happy path. Rookie pitfalls: feature overload (5 unpolished features) · hours lost on login screens · demos crashing on edge bugs.
- One-page solution canvas: User → Input → Core logic / AI → Output → Feedback; and Problem · User · Solution · Top 3 features · Tech · Impact.
- "Hour 14 is where most teams quit. That is your edge." Shrink the problem · ask for help · ship the ugly version. "Be the clearest thinker."
- The 3-minute pitch: Hook 0:30 · Who and why 0:30 · Live demo 1:15 · How it works 0:30 · Ask 0:15. Open with a person · demo the happy path, skip edge cases, keep a recorded backup · end with one specific ask.
- Live demo principles: pre-record a backup video · show value in 30 seconds (skip onboarding forms) · stick to the scripted path.
